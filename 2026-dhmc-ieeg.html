Born in 2014

E. Bannier, G. Barker, V. Borghesani, ..., Y. O. Halchenko, ..., H. Zhu. The Open Brain Consent: Informing research participants and obtaining consent to share brain imaging data. Human Brain Mapping, feb 2021. doi: 10.1002/hbm.25351

Federally regulated but locally “managed”

Over 20 contributors · 8 translations of the Ultimate Form · IRB committees can use OBC instead of reviewing ad-hoc wording — every archive downstream (OpenNeuro, DANDI, NEMAR, EMBER) relies on consent like this at the gate

The ask: plan for depositing to the archive in the consent form

Consent is the one step that is MUCH harder if not impossible to do later!

“We will give access to the data we are collecting, including the imaging data, to the general public via the Internet and a fully open database.”
— one sentence from the OBC Ultimate form

Ideally aim to

  • Name the archives, not just “investigators at other institutions”: OpenNeuro/NEMAR, DANDI, EMBER
  • Open as the default tier; controlled access as the fallback, not the only option
  • Permit secondary research beyond this protocol's aims, with no end date
  • Cover the whole record: iEEG + electrode-localization CT/MRI + task logs + video
  • Take care about data (pseudo)annonymization as early as feasible

Reuse vetted wording the way you reuse a software license — your IRB then reviews a known template instead of ad-hoc prose: open-brain-consent.readthedocs.io · NIH sample language for secondary research (2022) · it also covers the sharing half of your NIH DMS plan

NWB: one file format for all modalities + metadata

Neurodata Without Borders: open standard for neurophysiology data (spikes, LFP, behavior, imaging, ...) in a single self-describing, HDF5- or Zarr-backed file — data and metadata travel together.

NWB supports a range of user experience

Pick the point on the automated ↔ flexible scale that fits: GUIDE (no code, GUI), NeuroConv (low code, common formats), PyNWB / MatNWB (custom code against the NWB APIs).

NWB GUIDE: the TurboTax of file conversions

github.com/NeurodataWithoutBorders/nwb-guide

No-code desktop app, step by step like tax software: project structure → data entry (source data, file metadata) → conversion (validate, preview, review) → upload to DANDI. Screenshot: the File Metadata step for one session, Neuropixels device and electrode groups prefilled from the source data.

In large part thanks to apps such as Neurosift

  • Browser-based NWB viewer: neurosift.app
    • no download required
    • integrates with most major archives (DANDI, EMBER, OpenNeuro)
    • works on TB-sized files "just as well" as MB-sized ones
  • Free & Open-source (fix & extend at will)
    • repo: github.com/flatironinstitute/neurosift
    • JOSS: doi:10.21105/joss.06590
  • New event-related signals view
    • align any time series to any column of a trials/intervals table, pick channels, group trials by a condition
  • New spectrogram view

Example 1: behavior, grouped by condition

Camera motion energy, aligned to auditory_cue_time, grouped by gabor_stimulus_contrast — DANDI:000409
Zador Lab, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory · Mus musculus · visual stimulus + decision-making task

Solid lines are per-group means, dashed are spread, faint lines are individual trials. Motion energy dips into the cue, then rebounds after it — and the rebound separates by stimulus contrast. Everything here is streamed from DANDI; nothing downloaded.

Example 2: LFP aligned to reward

LFP aligned to reward_start_time, channels 0, 2, 4 — DANDI:001539
Jadhav Lab, Brandeis University · Long Evans rat · odor–place association

Trials without a reward time are skipped and reported in the title ("68 of 95 intervals; 27 without reward_start_time"). Clear evoked deflection right after reward onset on every selected channel.

Example 3: LFP around stimulus presentations

40 Hz pulse-train presentations, probe D LFP, channels 5, 22, 25, 32 — DANDI:001637
Allen Institute for Neural Dynamics · Mus musculus · predictive coding

Any intervals table works, not just "trials" — here the stimulus-presentation table. Transient at pulse-train onset, then a slower negative deflection around 1 s.

Example 4: spectrogram of an LFP trace

/processing/ecephys/LFP/v_trace, 0–100 Hz, ~2 min of a 200 Hz recording — DANDI:001347
Karl Deisseroth Lab, Stanford University · human neural response to an eyepuff

Spectrogram view: DPSS multitaper (NW 3, K 5), 1024-sample windows with 50% overlap, 60 Hz notch, 1/f whitening, magma colormap. Everything is computed in the browser from the streamed data. The link currently points at a Neurosift preview build.

Example 5: spectrogram, no normalization

/processing/ecephys/LFP/LFP, channels 6–8 averaged, 0–625 Hz, first 30 s of a 1250 Hz recording — DANDI:000059

Same view with different settings: Hann window (512 samples, 50% overlap), 60 Hz notch, raw dB (no normalization), viridis colormap, full 0–625 Hz range. The link currently points at a Neurosift preview build.

Carrots, not (only) sticks

Figure from M. Hanke, "Carrots! Not sticks!" hedgedoc.psychoinformatics.de/p/gBE63_ytiE

What pulls people toward standards

  • Carrots — the standards themselves: free tools, free archive storage, "someone else already solved this", collaborators who can actually read your data
  • Sticks exist too — mostly from funders and journals: NIH Data Management & Sharing Policy, journal data-availability requirements
  • Our pitch today is almost entirely carrots — the stick is already pulling on everyone; we're here to make the carrot side concrete
