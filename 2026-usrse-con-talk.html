BIDS as a meta-standard

Not a wire format — a contract. 11 modalities · 25 active BEPs · a machine-readable schema · a validator · turnkey BIDS-Apps — all hang off it.

What a usable standard is made of

  • One layout, declared — sub-<label>/[ses-<label>/]<datatype>/. You have seen one BIDS dataset, you have seen them all.
  • File names that parse — entities in a fixed order, not prose
  • A short list of allowed formats per data type, instead of “whatever wrote it”
  • Metadata with a place to live — JSON sidecars, TSV tables, and an inheritance principle so it is said once
  • Readable by both — a prose specification for humans and a machine-readable schema the validator, the docs and the tooling are generated from
  • An amendment process — BEPs and open PR review, so the standard can be wrong and still recover

None of this is neuroimaging. It is the anatomy of a standard your field can adopt — and the contract an agent can be held to.

Cost: harnesses, harnesses, harnesses

Each automation is itself code — CI YAML, hooks, nightly cron, deploy scripts, glue. Someone maintains that.

By 2024 the harness was bigger than several of the projects under it.
That is why a small RSE center was dragged toward AI assistance — not for the science, but for the maintenance of the meta-layer.

Verbally: the five verbs also map onto the SciOps maturity ladder (Johnson et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.00077 -- I am a co-author on it) — L1 ad-hoc to L3 open-source ecosystems (BIDS/NWB/DataLad exemplars) to L4 SciOps pipelines (Automate) to L5 Optimizing with AI. Most labs sit at L1-L2. The 5 verbs are the practical climb to L3-L4; AI is what makes L5 feasible for a small RSE center.

con/skills + con/yolo: we automate the harness itself.

  • The sandbox is the only piece that enforces self-containment — inside a fresh container, what you did not declare is simply not there. The property gets checked on every run instead of being a virtue you remember.
  • Pre-flight, then run. Assert every credential, token and access path before the job starts — fail in the first second, not in hour three with half a dataset written.
The sandbox line is the same move as a validator: turn a discipline into something the machine fails on. Pre-flight is John Lee's point. Caveat if asked: approvals granted interactively on first use -- a hook authorising against an external service -- cannot be checked ahead of time, so pre-flight covers what is checkable and the rest still bites you.
