We compare two ways to deploy ORINOCO, a system for collaboratively managing and reusing structured research metadata.

Dedicated services or GitHub pull requests: curate people, projects and publications together, then reuse their relationships across lab operations.

Deployment 1 · Dedicated services

ORINOCO

Developed by Michael Hanke and colleagues in the Psychoinformatics group, INM-7, Forschungszentrum Jülich. Shared LinkML schemas connect records and drive forms and validation.

Contributorssubmit records→ Service inboxcurators review→ Collectionquery and reuse

What is the Dump Things Service?

A metadata API that validates incoming records, stages submissions for review, and serves curated records for reuse.

Operating choice: maintain the service, its storage and access controls.

Deployment 2 · GitHub collaboration

ORINOCO Lite at CON

We retain ORINOCO’s schemas and processing tools, adapting their operation to YAML records in Git, pull-request review and automated website builds.

Pull requestspeople review→ YAML in Gitmerge records→ Actionsvalidate + build

AI proposes. People curate.

Source adapters and AI can prepare changes; schemas constrain their structure, while reviewers decide identity, attribution and meaning.

Operating choice: maintain GitHub workflows, static hosting and the editing App.

One collection · Multiple uses

Follow the relationships

Shared identifiers link people to projects and research outputs. The same structured information supports discovery, public communication and reporting.

Beyond the website: a funder report

TRR379 already uses this approach for its DFG annual survey. Its central metadata infrastructure gathers personnel information and generates the funder’s census PDF.

Keep the records reusable

Keep structured records in Git. Pin separate components with submodules; opt into git-annex for large media in site-specific content.

Released packages bundle rendering assets; ordinary site builds need no upstream git-annex checkout.
