 - M - standardization (of (meta)data) can be critical to establish the framework for mutual HI & AI knowledge storage
   - machine (not AI) and human readable
   - unlike loose .md's - deterministically validatable
   - facilitates all FAIR (all are about (meta)data), in particular 
   - that is why we (CON) heavily involved in standards (BIDS, NWB, HED, DICOM, ...)
 - with common M, can get range of C(ontrollers), e.g. see all the BIDS tools
 - for the same M, can be a range of V(iewers), facilitating use-case specifics

- "aspirational" vs "pragmatic"
  - NIDM vs BIDS --> BIDS with TermURL ...  BEP028: BIDS-PROV
  - W3C PROV vs DataLad run/containers-run  --> PROV export
  - idea: siloed 'ad-hoc' implementations/schemas are seemingly easier (overfit the use-case) and could be considered to be "pragmatic" in the short(er) term but ideally adoption of common standard etc allows for longevity etc
    - 'agreeing to agree' is 'hard': social aspect
    - staged 'implementation': start with "inspired" aligned, proceed to *EPs, consider adopting "pluggable" standards (like NWB extensions)
  - some middle grounds could be adopting 
    - common languages to specify metadata schemas -- LinkML, developed just a few miles north
    - already existing schemas and ontologies (schema.org; datacite...) 
    - existing formalizations for e.g. 
      - license descriptions (REUSE - https://github.com/con/skills/tree/master/introduce-reuse-compliance), 
      - DUO  -- data use ontology
      - STAMPED -- "language"/dimensions for research object
    - ... git / git-annex ... ?

- interesting effects on AI use

  - breaking out of silos -- there is a better chance to discover similar/related developments or facts to support arguments in other fields, which otherwise you would not be aware of.
    - can cite that paper showing how silo'ed neuroscience is!
    - hence speaking here and showing CON and BIDS etc

- ideas to circulate / point to

  - AI is "expensive" in terms of resources.
  - we need to "collaborate" more to not redo (less of "ephemeral" software), and also just "do once".  my example -- "codespell" cruisade. My AI spends my tokens once to do proper/thorough codespell' fixup. Quite tricky for elderly/big/specialized (like UBERON ontology) projects without AI!
  
  - so we should mandate our AIs to do research before jumping into implementing new stuff
